Important information

Read before relying on this tool: this is an engineering screening aid, not a legal certificate of authenticity. It can only run the checks that are technically possible from a single photo/scan in a browser — number-format & checksum rules, QR/MRZ decoding, cross-field logic, and image-forensic tamper detection. It cannot reach UIDAI, ECI, Passport Seva, Sarathi/mParivahan, or board (CBSE/state) servers, so it can never prove a document is genuine — at best it can say the format and available checks are consistent, and route anything uncertain to a human reviewer. Genuine documents can still be expired, damaged, or from an older valid format — none of that makes them fake. Full Aadhaar numbers are masked everywhere in this tool's output and storage.

Indian Government Document Authenticity Screener

Upload an Aadhaar, Passport, EPIC/Voter ID, Driving Licence, or Class X/XII marksheet. The pipeline classifies the document, runs OCR, decodes any QR/MRZ present, applies issuer-specific format & checksum rules, checks cross-field consistency, runs image-forensic tamper analysis, and produces an explainable, evidence-tiered risk decision — with the exact reason behind every flag.

How results are decided (decision reference)
DecisionMeaning
LOW RISK — NOT VERIFIEDRisk 0–20. Every check this browser could run was consistent. This is deliberately not called "authentic": no authoritative or cryptographic verification was performed, so nothing here can establish genuineness.
REVIEW REQUIREDRisk 21–45, or any higher score where analysis confidence is under 55%. Evidence is mixed, incomplete, or unreadable — send to a human reviewer, don't auto-approve or auto-reject.
SUSPICIOUSRisk 46–70 with enough analysis confidence to mean it. Several independent indicators point the same way.
HIGH RISKRisk 71–100. A hard rule failed on a field read at high confidence and forensic evidence agrees. A rule that failed on a badly-read field can never reach this band on its own.

Evidence hierarchy this tool follows (strongest to weakest — visual similarity alone never decides a case):

  1. Cryptographic / issuer signature — e.g. UIDAI Secure QR signature, e-passport chip PKI. Not verifiable client-side; always reported as unavailable, never assumed.
  2. Authoritative database lookup — e.g. electoral roll, Sarathi/mParivahan, board/DigiLocker. Also not available offline; flagged as such.
  3. Machine-readable consistency — Aadhaar Verhoeff checksum, passport MRZ check digits, decoded QR payload vs. visible fields. This tool checks these directly.
  4. Cross-field consistency — date chronology, marksheet totals vs. subject marks, format plausibility.
  5. Image forensics — error-level analysis for local editing/compositing.
  6. Visual/layout similarity — fonts, spacing, colour. Weakest evidence; used only as a minor supporting signal, never a sole reason to flag.

Four outcomes are used for every individual check:

  • PASS — the rule ran and the document satisfied it.
  • FAIL — the rule ran, the document failed it, and the field behind it was read confidently enough to believe the result.
  • UNCERTAIN — the rule ran and did not pass, but the field was read too poorly to tell a forgery from a misread. This adds no risk; it lowers analysis confidence.
  • NOT AVAILABLE — the check cannot be performed in a browser at all (issuer databases, cryptographic signatures, passport chips), or the field was never found.

Risk and analysis confidence are separate numbers. Risk answers "how strong is the evidence that something is wrong?". Analysis confidence answers "how much should you trust that risk number?". A high risk score with low analysis confidence is reported as REVIEW REQUIRED, never as a conclusion.
